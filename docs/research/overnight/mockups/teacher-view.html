“A tax on a polluting good gets rid of the external cost completely.”
Part: Negative externalities and indirect taxes · about half of those offered picked it · 6–9 students (low evidence)
Starter MCQ for the next lesson

A tax equal to the external cost is put on a polluting good. What happens to pollution?

  1. It falls to zero, because the tax covers the full external cost
  2. It falls to the social optimum, where some pollution is still made
  3. It stays the same, because firms pass all of the tax to buyers
  4. It rises, because the government spends the tax revenue on output

Answer B. A, C and D each come from a wrong idea students hold.

“Price elasticity of demand is the slope of the demand curve.”
Part: Price elasticity of demand · most of those offered picked it · 10–14 students
Starter MCQ for the next lesson

Along a straight, downward-sloping demand curve, how does PED change?

  1. It stays the same, because the slope is the same all along it
  2. It is more elastic at high prices and less elastic at low prices
  3. It is unit elastic all along it, because the line is straight
  4. It is more elastic at low prices, where people buy more of it

Answer B. A, C and D each come from a wrong idea students hold.
