# Personalisation in Synapse: what the evidence supports

Evidence review for Synapse, an MCP learning service for Edexcel A-level Economics A (9EC0), used by students aged 16-18 in the UK. The server never calls an AI model. Every personalisation is a fixed rule in `settings.toml`, applied to labels the school's chatbot sends. This review asks which rules are backed by research, which are not, and what must hold for the rules to be safe for under-18s.

Companion files:
- [`settings-proposal.toml`](settings-proposal.toml): proposed values, using the same key names as `settings.toml`.
- [`personalisation-findings.csv`](personalisation-findings.csv): all 134 kept findings.

Marks: ✓ = a blind verifier confirmed the claim against the source. ~ = the claim is broadly supported, but something is off (what is off is stated). No finding was marked ✗ or left unverified, so none were dropped. "Our choice" marks a number that no source gives.

## 1. Summary

The personalisations worth building, ranked by evidence strength × fit to a rules-only chat server × low effort:

1. **Explain every error.** Feedback scaled to the student's state, with no praise. This has the strongest learning evidence (feedback meta-analyses) and is a text change.
2. **Fade support only on trustworthy evidence.** Change one open-answer setting so that a single lenient chat reply can no longer mark a part secure. Supported by expertise-reversal and BKT analyses. Settings only.
3. **Exam-anchored spacing, the right way round.** Synapse's review shares currently run opposite to Cepeda et al. (2008). Also, immediate evidence should not grow the half-life. RCT-backed. Settings only.
4. **Optional one-tap confidence on MCQ checks.** Used only to choose how much feedback to give and when to re-check. Lab learning evidence, plus UK feasibility data.
5. **Relevance done right.** The student says where an idea shows up in their life. Interests stay as context only. The evidence is mostly on interest, with grade gains in low-confidence subgroups.

Later: system-initiated checks when engagement drops (engagement evidence only), and a per-student learning-rate offset (prediction evidence only, off by default).

Avoid emotion detection, growth-mindset messages and praise. Do not infer interests.

## 2. Recommended features (top 5)

Outcome labels used below: **L** = learning (test or grade), **I** = interest or motivation, **E** = engagement or usage, **P** = prediction accuracy only.

### 2.1 Explain every error, scaled to the learner's state (rank 1)

- **Evidence.**
  - High-information feedback d = 0.99 vs corrective-only feedback 0.46 (L); effects on motivation are smaller than on learning: 3-01 ✓.
  - In computer-based learning, elaborated feedback 0.49 > knowledge of the correct answer 0.32 > right/wrong only 0.05 (L): 3-02 ~ (abstract only).
  - A network meta-analysis ranks elaborated feedback as most likely the best type (L): 3-03 ~ (abstract only).
  - Low achievers get directive feedback with scaffolding; for high achievers, verification may be enough; little or no praise (guidance): 3-04 ✓.
  - Over a third of feedback effects are negative, and effects weaken when feedback points at the self (L): 3-05 ~.
  - Effort-praise replications were null (I): 4-12 ~.
  - Timing does not matter on average (g = 0.03, L): 3-06 ✓. Synapse's immediate feedback costs nothing.
- **Signal.** The answer score, the misconception picked, and `p_now`.
- **Plan / settings.**
  - Every wrong answer gets a one-line reason plus the correct idea. A bare "incorrect" is never allowed: `[feedback] explain_on_error = true`.
  - A correct answer on a weak part still gets one line on why it is right.
  - A short confirmation alone is used only when `p_now >= feedback.verify_only_from` (0.75, our choice) and the student said they were sure.
  - Feedback text names which points were produced and which are missing. It has no person praise and no peer comparison.
- **Expected effect.** Small to moderate gains in learning on checked items. The meta-analytic effects are a ceiling: the studies are mixed in age, and none are chat-based.
- **Risk.** Long explanations go unread. Mitigation: keep within the existing word caps (`points.max_words` 80).

### 2.2 Fade support only on trustworthy evidence (rank 2)

- **Evidence.**
  - Novices learn more with high assistance (d = 0.505), knowledgeable learners with less (d = −0.428). Helping novices matters more (L): 2-01 ~ (abstract only); 7a-16 ~.
  - Adapting to level added to the effect of interest-matching and feedback (0.35 vs 0.13, L): 1-11 ~, 6-15 ✓.
  - Fading worked examples on self-explanation performance beat fixed fading (L): 2-04 ✓.
  - With guess 0.10, one correct open reply takes a part from 0.30 to 0.815, which is past "secure" (model calculation): 7a-04 ✓.
  - The threshold and which evidence counts matter more than how sophisticated the model is (P): 2-15 ✓.
  - Difficulties become undesirable without the background to answer: 2-14 ✓.
  - Population models pass slow learners early (simulation): 6-11 ✓, 7a-13 ~.
- **Signal.** `p_now`, `n_evidence`, and the evidence type per part.
- **Plan / settings.**
  - Open-answer guess 0.10 → **0.20** for `explain_back`, `past_question`, `link` and `extend`. Two correct replies are then needed for secure: 0.30 → 0.69 → 0.92.
  - `support.min_evidence_for_fade = 2`: support never drops below hint until two pieces of evidence exist.
  - `support.fade_requires`: explain-back-only support needs one correct non-immediate open answer, not MCQs alone.
  - MCQ guess = 1 / number of options shown.
  - Transfer-mode reviews only after a correct latest answer.
  - A startup check that every evidence type keeps G + S < 1 and learn rate < 1 − S/(1−G) (7a-03 ~).
  - `levels.secure` stays 0.75. The guess change alone stops one-reply mastery, and raising both would double-count.
- **Expected effect.** Fewer false "secure" badges and fewer premature fades, especially for slower learners. Students who learn fast need about one extra correct answer.
- **Risk.**
  - Slower progress for able students. Mitigation: the expertise-reversal asymmetry says this costs less than under-supporting novices. Tune on post-secure review failures.
  - Students who struggle with writing may stay at hint longer. Mitigation: monitor by prior band.

### 2.3 Exam-anchored spacing, the right way round (rank 3)

- **Evidence.**
  - The optimal restudy gap falls from 43% to 8% of the retention interval as the interval grows. As a share of days left to the exam, that is 0.30 (near) down to 0.07 (far). Optimal vs zero gap gave d = 1.1 (L): 7a-14 ✓.
  - Reviewing when predicted recall reaches 0.86-0.94 beat low thresholds and conventional spacing, but did not differ from 0.70 (L, adults, word pairs): 2-12 ~, 7b-03 ~.
  - A success straight after study barely grows memory stability: 7a-09 ✓.
  - Quizzing gains grow with repetitions, and are smaller for business/economics (g = 0.31) (L): 7b-16 ~.
  - Three spaced relearning sessions are efficient for concepts (L): 7b-17 ~.
  - Personalised review beat one-size spacing over a semester (+10% at 28 days, L, RCT): 8-08 ✓.
- **Signal.** Days to exam, decayed `p_now`, and the evidence type.
- **Plan / settings.**
  - `scheduling.share_far/mid/near` from 0.25 / 0.15 / 0.08 to **0.10 / 0.19 / 0.30**.
  - New `scheduling.review_when_below = 0.80`, our choice. It sits inside the 0.70-0.94 plateau and adds less review load than 0.85. `p_now` is p(known), not a recall probability.
  - New `decay.on_success_immediate = 1.0`.
  - New `scheduling.one_review_per_part_per_session = true`.
- **Expected effect.** Better delayed retention for the same review count. The direction is RCT-backed; the exact values are low confidence, since the evidence is from adults learning trivia.
- **Risk.** Fewer reviews in the last fortnight could feel like under-support to students who cram. Mitigation: `review_when_below` still catches decaying parts; `min_gap_days` stays 1.

### 2.4 Optional one-tap confidence on MCQ checks (rank 4)

- **Evidence.**
  - Feedback roughly doubled recall of correct answers given as guesses (.85 vs .40, L, lab): 3-07 ~. The verifier notes that Exp 1 tested in the same session.
  - Confident errors are corrected more readily, but uncorrected ones come back after a week (L, lab): 3-08 ✓.
  - Confidence mostly proxies prior knowledge, so it should not enter the update: 3-09 ✓.
  - UK Eedi data (7,302 students aged 6-16): optional ratings tracked accuracy; boys were more confident at equal accuracy and disadvantaged students less (correlational): 3-10 ✓.
  - Asking for confidence in English schools did not change attainment (d = −0.02): 3-11 ✓.
  - Wording with the word "confident" can affect low-confidence people: 3-13 ~.
- **Signal.** Optional `answer.confidence` ∈ {sure, not sure, guessing}.
- **Plan / settings.** `[confidence]`:
  - Ask at most every second check (`ask_every = 2`), never when the session is fatigued.
  - Wording: "How likely is this right?"
  - Correct + unsure/guessing → one line on why it is right.
  - Wrong + sure → name the misconception, then a re-check after `recheck_gap_days = 2`.
  - Correct + sure → short confirmation.
  - No `p_known` change from confidence, ever.
- **Expected effect.** Better retention of shaky correct answers and fewer returning confident misconceptions. The rating itself is not an intervention (3-11).
- **Risk.**
  - Fairness: confidence differs by gender and disadvantage. Mitigation: confidence never lowers `p_known`, never gates content, and is never shown to teachers or used to rank students.
  - Reactivity. Mitigation: the tap is optional and infrequent.

### 2.5 Relevance done right (rank 5)

- **Evidence.**
  - A relevance writing task raised interest and grades for low-expectation students (I + L): 1-05 ~.
  - Utility-value writing cut a grade gap by 61% (d = 0.55 for the target group, L, college): 1-07 ✓.
  - Lowest performers benefited most (L): 1-08 ~.
  - Classroom relevance raised value beliefs; achievement rose only in the quotations version (I + L): 1-09 ~.
  - Telling low-confidence learners that material is useful backfired, while self-generated relevance helped (I + L, lab): 1-06 ~.
  - Depth must match: 1-03 ~.
  - AI-personalised examples raised motivation but not learning (I only): 1-12 ~.
  - LLM class-level interest contexts were rated low and needed fixing (I only): 1-13 ~.
  - Decorative interesting details harmed transfer (L): 1-14 ~.
- **Signal.** The stated interest (`style.interests`), a one-question `interest_depth`, `p_now`, and the stuck/fatigue state.
- **Plan / settings.**
  - Optional `relate_to_me` step, at most once per session (`relevance.max_per_session = 1`). Offered only after evidence exists, preferred when the part is new or stuck, never when fatigued. Unscored.
  - Offered to every student, never targeted by background.
  - Guard text: the chatbot does not tell students why something matters to them; it asks.
  - The interest must carry the economic mechanism, not decorate it.
  - Prefer everyday contexts over careers.
  - Interests never change the move or the support level.
  - `style.interests_enabled` switch (default true). Interests stay empty until the student sets them.
- **Expected effect.** Mainly interest and motivation. Possible grade benefits for low-confidence students, from college and secondary studies. Do not claim learning gains from interest-based examples.
- **Risk.**
  - Relies on chatbot compliance, which the server cannot check.
  - A nagging feel. Mitigation: the cap and the skip option.
  - Stereotyping. Mitigation: stated interests only, never inferred.

### Runners-up (build after the top 5)

- **System-initiated checks when engagement drops** (E only):
  - When the passive + "just show me" share is ≥ 0.5 over the signal window, use MCQ checks instead of open explain-back (5-03 ~).
  - After 4 passive labels, offer 2-3 ways to continue (4-01 ✓, 4-07 ✓). Choice raises motivation, not later learning (d = 0.10 n.s.).
  - After 2 "show" requests on a part, give the first step as a two-option MCQ (5-04 ✓).
- **Per-student learning-rate offset** (P only): see §7.

## 4. Myths to avoid

- **Learning styles.** Not used anywhere in Synapse (`learner-model.md`). No workstream found support for matching instruction to styles.
- **"Students learn best at 85% correct."** It is a theorem about binary classifiers with no human data (Wilson et al. 2019; 2-11 ✓, 7b-02 ✓). Rosenshine's ~80% for guided practice is correlational classroom guidance (7b-01 ✓).
- **"There is an evidence-based mastery cut-off such as 0.95."** It is a tutor convention. Thresholds are application-specific (Pelánek & Řihák 2017; 2-15 ✓, 7a-15 ✓).
- **"Swapping in a favourite topic raises learning."** Depth must match (1-03 ~). Personalised examples raised motivation but not learning (Leong et al. 2024; 1-12 ~).
- **"More interest means more learning."** Interest and learning are separate outcomes (1-12 ~, 1-14 ~).
- **"Telling students why a topic matters motivates them."** It can backfire for low-confidence learners (Canning & Harackiewicz 2015; 1-06 ~).
- **"Short growth-mindset messages raise grades" and "praise effort, not ability."** Near-null in the best studies and in a UK trial (4-10 ~, 4-11 ✓, 4-12 ~).
- **"Giving students choice improves learning."** It raises motivation; later learning d = 0.10, n.s. (Patall et al. 2008; 4-07 ✓).
- **"Frustration and confusion should be removed."** Boredom is the more harmful state (Baker et al. 2010; Pardos et al. 2014; 4-01 ✓, 4-02 ✓).
- **"AI can read students' emotions from chat."** No validated benchmark; log detectors are weak (4-03 ✓).
- **"Asking for confidence raises attainment."** d = −0.02 in English schools (Foster 2022; 3-11 ✓).
- **"Correct answers need no feedback" and "one correction fixes a confident misconception."** Both wrong (3-07 ~, 3-08 ✓).
- **"Delayed feedback is better for secondary students."** A subgroup result that did not survive meta-regression (Kandemir, Esposito, Gurgand & Ramus 2026; 3-06 ✓).
- **"Mertens et al. 2022 is a timing meta-analysis."** It is about feedback type (3-03 ~).
- **"Students stopped using Khanmigo when it refused answers."** Not supported by the trial: use was thin from the start (Oreopoulos & Low 2026; 5-03 ~). The row in `docs/build/reference/research.md` needs correcting.
- **"AI tutors double learning."** One immediate post-test with Harvard undergraduates (Kestin et al. 2025; 5-06 ~).
- **"Guardrailed GPT tutors improve exam results."** The guardrails prevented harm; they did not create gains (Bastani et al. 2025; 5-01 ✓).
- **"Personalised learning raises high-school achievement."** Not significant in high school, and the design was quasi-experimental (RAND; 5-10 ~).
- **"Adaptive learning widens attainment gaps."** No direct evidence found (ws-6). Still monitor by prior band (5-07 ~, 2-08 ✓).
- **"FSRS needs 400-1,000 reviews."** Out of date: the current optimiser fits in stages with shrinkage (8-10 ~). The FSRS note in `research.md` needs updating.
- **"Individualised BKT improves learning."** The evidence is for prediction or simulation; no RCT isolates per-student parameters (8-01 ✓, 8-04 ~).
- **"Optimal spacing is a fixed share of time to the test."** The share falls as the interval grows (Cepeda et al. 2008; 7a-14 ✓).
- **"MCQ guess = 1/n is a fitted value."** It is an assumption (7a-05 ✓).
- **"Retrieval practice works equally for economics."** The business/economics subgroup is smaller: g = 0.31 vs 0.50 overall (Yang et al. 2021; 7b-16 ~).
- **"Attention drops after 10 minutes."** Not supported by primary data (secondary source only, ws-7b notes; not a finding row).
- **"Our contract says processor, so the Children's code does not apply."** The contract label does not decide the role (6-02 ✓).

## 5. Privacy rules personalisation must follow (from workstream 6)

Checklist:
- [ ] Every processing purpose is written into the school's instructions. Synapse acts only on them (6-02 ✓).
- [ ] Core profiling has no toggle. The core exception is read narrowly, and profiling purposes are never bundled (6-01 ✓).
- [ ] Non-core profiling has its own setting, **off by default** (6-01 ✓, 4-16 ✓).
- [ ] No pooling across schools and no tuning settings on live pupil data. Either would make Synapse a controller (6-02 ✓, 6-03 ✓).
- [ ] Cross-student estimates within one school only on a written instruction: aggregate only, minimum cell size 5, default `w_cross = 0` (6-02 ✓; ws-6 §4).
- [ ] A/B comparisons (the `minimal` profile, an interests on/off cohort, personalisation on/off) count as non-core research. They need an explicit school instruction, a research basis and student information (6-03 ✓; ws-6 §4).
- [ ] No inferred emotion. Distress is routed to the chatbot vendor and the DSL, not into the learner model (4-15 ~, 6-09 ✓).
- [ ] No SEND, EAL or demographic data in Synapse. Per-student accuracy metrics are exported so the school can do its own slicing (6-12 ~).
- [ ] No grade predictions or single course score. Teacher views show evidence count, recency and uncertainty. Students can contest a level via `check_me`, and rebuild re-scores after corrections (6-05 ✓, 6-06 ✓).
- [ ] Transparency for 16-17-year-olds: `get_my_progress` explains levels, misconceptions, review reasons and what is recorded. A just-in-time notice appears when a non-core use switches on (6-07 ✓).
- [ ] Stopping is offered neutrally, with progress kept. No streaks or rewards tied to session length (6-10 ✓).
- [ ] A DPIA before deployment, covering inferred data, the core/non-core map, bias, child harms, vendor dependencies and DPO sign-off (6-08 ~, 6-04 ✓).
- [ ] Personal traits (learning speed, confidence) are never shown as labels to students or teachers (8-06 ~, 3-10 ✓).

| Fatigue/usage shown to teachers | Non-core | off; minutes and offloading rate only if turned on |
| Affect inference | Not built | n/a |

**Conflicts resolved.**
- `review_when_below`: 0.80 (ws-2) vs 0.85 (ws-7b). We take 0.80, because thresholds of 0.70-0.94 did not differ in the source, and 0.80 adds less load.
- `levels.secure`: 0.85 (7a-15) vs 0.75 (2-15). We keep 0.75 and raise the open-answer guess instead. Doing both would double-count. Keeping 0.75 also avoids a gap between `secure` and `queue_band`'s upper end of 0.75.
- MCQ option count: ws-7b proposed 3. We keep 4, because option count was never tested for learning; the guess still follows the options actually shown.
- A 3-option MCQ (guess 0.33) would be discounted less than `immediate` (0.30). The max rule fixes this.

**Degeneracy check on the proposed values** (run in review):
- Inputs: slip raised by fatigue (+0.15, capped at 0.40), and the per-student learn-rate ceiling of 1.5× in odds (0.143).
- Every type keeps G + S < 1. The largest is 0.58, for a 3-option MCQ.
- Every type keeps learn rate < 1 − S/(1−G). The tightest bound is 0.56, for `prequestion`. At the 0.40 cap with G = 0.33 the bound is 0.40, still above 0.143.

**Fatigue false alarms.** These are computed assuming expected score = `p_now`. Surprise uses `p_now`, not the guess/slip-adjusted expected success, which gives a drift of about ±0.07. That is as large as a real fatigue drop (about −0.05). Log calls before tuning (7b-08 ~).

All k values and caps are our choice. No source tests the data volume at which a personal estimate starts to beat the population one.

The bounds of [0.5, 1.5] are deliberately narrower than the source's spread (about 0.5× to 4×, 8-07 ~). Over-estimating a learner's rate means fewer checks, which locks the error in (7a-13). So under-crediting fast learners is the safer mistake.

**Data quality and behaviour.**
- Log the chatbot client. A lenient client inflates surprise, so compare across clients before enabling.
- "Untested" reviews stay independent of the offset.
- The offset is internal and never shown as "fast" or "slow".
- It is a pure function of earlier events, so `rebuild_state` stays deterministic.

**Cohort priors, reconciled with privacy (ws-6).**
- A class-level starting estimate uses classmates' data, which is cross-student profiling. It goes through the existing `difficulty.w_cross` hook, which stays **0 by default**.
- It is turned on only when all of these hold:
  1. The school gives a written instruction naming it, for its own pupils (6-02 ✓).
  2. The data come from one school only, never pooled across schools (6-02 ✓, 6-03 ✓).
  3. The estimate is an aggregate per-part first-attempt success rate, with a minimum cell size of 5 (6-01 ✓; ws-6 §4).
  4. The DPIA lists it, and a just-in-time notice tells students that their class's results shape their starting point (6-07 ✓, 6-08 ~).
  5. It is capped downward at 0.075, so a strong student in a low set is not given a low expectation. It shrinks quickly as the student's own evidence arrives.
- Without all five, there is no cohort prior.

**Evaluation.**
- `personalisation.enabled = false` by default. The only randomised evidence for pooled personal models is on retention (8-08 ✓); everything else measures prediction.
- Comparing on and off is an A/B research use (ws-6: non-core). It runs only with an explicit school instruction, a research basis and student information.
- Outcome: delayed retention on unseen past-paper items (2-08 ✓, 2-09 ~), not prediction accuracy.

## Method

- **Workstreams.** Nine research workstreams ran in parallel:
  1. Interests and relevance.
  2. Prior knowledge and guidance.
  3. Feedback and metacognition.
  4. Motivation and affect.
  5. AI tutors.
  6. Safety, privacy and fairness.
  7. Settings values. Workstream 7 had **two researchers**: 7a covered the learner model and 7b session and teaching.
  8. Population to personal estimates.
- **Researchers.** Each researcher searched primary sources, wrote notes (`notes/ws-*.md`) and returned structured findings.
- **Verification.** The verifier role was run as **one blind verifier per workstream**, in parallel. Each verifier saw only the claims and sources, not the researcher's reasoning, and marked each claim ✓, ~ or ✗, with corrected claims and numbers where needed (`notes/verified-ws-*.json`).
- **Synthesis.** This document drops ✗ rows (there were none), keeps ✓ and ~ rows with what is off, and uses the verifier's corrections.
- **Outcome types.** Learning, interest, engagement and prediction are kept separate.
- **Numbers.** Every number either comes from a cited source or is marked "our choice".
- **Limits.** No source studied UK students aged 16-18 in A-level economics through a chat tutor. The closest are the UK Eedi studies with ages 6-16 (3-10, 5-05), English secondary schools (3-11), and the EEF trials. Many ~ rows rest on abstracts only.
