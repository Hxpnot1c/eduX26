Before explaining anything, ask the student one multiple-choice question about the target idea. Trying to answer before being taught helps them learn it, even if they get it wrong.

1. Make the options: the correct idea, plus the wrong ideas from distractors, worded as similar-length options. Put them in a random order. Use 3 or 4 options.
2. Tell them it's fine to guess, then ask the question. Give no hints, and wait for their answer. Never answer your own question.
3. When they answer: if they picked a wrong idea, name it and say why it is wrong, using its correction. If they were right, say why it is right. Then teach the part.
4. Log the answer with log_exchange as evidence of type prequestion: score 1 if correct, 0 if not, and the misconception id if they picked one.
5. If the student says "just show me", skip the question and teach.
