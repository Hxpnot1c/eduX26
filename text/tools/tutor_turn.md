Call this at the start of every reply, before writing anything (research questions about the world too). It records the student's message and answer, then returns the plan for your reply.
- message: the student's message, word for word.
- this_message: honest labels for the student's message; choice is skip_point when they say "I know this", or their pick from offer_choice (quiz, extend, stop). request_type is research for a factual or current question about the world, with its question_features, web_access (can you search the web here) and asks_recent (about recent data or events). Set source_conflict when they say a link failed or didn't say what was claimed, and pushback_changed when they challenged your last answer and you are changing it. Set asks_for_reviews when they ask to be quizzed or to revise without naming a topic. Set words to the number of words in their message (a count only), always.
- node_ids: optional part ids from the last plan or focus, when the message continues that topic.
- answer: only when the message answers what you asked last turn (see the server instructions); for a multiple-choice question, type mcq and choice, the letter picked. Never score it yourself.
- client: your name, e.g. claude, chatgpt or gemini.
Returns plan (steps to do in order; each move is explained in the server instructions), score_next (points to tick in their next answer), reference and focus.
Labelling rules (this_message and answer):
- request_type: research for a new question whose answer needs facts or figures about a particular place and time, or that asks for sources; their rewritten question for an Ask task is research too. A how or why question about a course idea is concept, even when it names a real country, currency or event. An answer to something you asked is never research: an answer to a course question, or "ok, go on", is concept; an answer to a source-checking task or a how-AI-works question is other, as is chat off the course.
- question_features: concept when the question uses an economics term or names an official statistic (inflation, CPI, unemployment rate, Bank Rate, real wages); the price of an everyday thing (petrol, rent, house prices) doesn't count on its own.
- source_conflict: true when they report that a source you gave would not open, or doesn't say what you said: the claim is missing, or its figure, date or period is different. Rounding of the same figure (9.1% vs about 9%) is not a conflict, and "it matches" is false. When their check shows you were wrong, also set conflict_flagged, not pushback_changed.
- engagement: passive with no question and no answer ("ok", "thanks"); active when they ask for something, or answer by recalling or restating; constructive when they add a step of their own: a reason ("because"), an inference ("so"), a comparison that reaches a conclusion, or applying the idea to a case. Being wrong doesn't change the level.
- points_met: tick a point only when the message contains all of it, in any words; half a point is not the point. On a source check (ail_verify), a is ticked when they say or show they looked (opened it, report what it says, or say it wouldn't load), and d when they give a verdict (matches, doesn't match, can't confirm); giving both figures without a verdict is c, not d. Never tick a point for words that only repeat your own reply, a point you just showed or your citation: on a source check that is not looking, in an explain-back it is copying.
